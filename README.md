# Neurotech-Afterimage

**What happens to neurotechnology when its developers move on, its trials stop, or its devices leave the market?**

Neurotech-Afterimage is a research archive about the lifecycle of neurotechnology. It documents clinical trial outcomes, company closures, discontinued devices, technology transfers, and questions about long-term patient support. The cases draw on public records, scientific publications, and attributed reporting.

## Why this project matters

Neurotechnology is often discussed in terms of new ideas, clinical results, and commercial milestones. Less attention goes to what happens when development stops, a company closes, a product is discontinued, or ownership changes.

Those transitions can matter for patients, clinicians, researchers, and the teams that inherit a technology. This archive preserves what can be established about them, along with the gaps that remain. It does not call every discontinued product a failure: the aim is to understand what happened and what future teams can learn from the record.

## Who is this archive for?

- **Researchers and academics** looking for clinical trial outcomes, evidence gaps, and historical context.
- **Founders and engineers** working through product discontinuation, handoffs, and long-term support.
- **Investors and analysts** tracing company transitions and commercialization history.
- **Clinicians and healthcare professionals** interested in device lifecycles and continuity of care.
- **Patients and advocacy groups** seeking documented information about discontinued technologies and support arrangements.

## Research limitations

This is a selective archive, not a complete census of neurotechnology projects. Each case relies on publicly accessible evidence, which can vary in detail, reliability, and proximity to the events described. The dossiers have not all received a complete, claim-by-claim factual audit.

When the available record leaves a question open, the dossier identifies it rather than filling the gap with speculation. The archive is for research and historical reference; it is not medical advice and does not guide individual treatment decisions.

## How to cite

When citing the archive, include its title, the repository URL, a version or commit identifier if available, and the date you accessed it. When citing a particular case, also cite the original sources listed in that dossier's evidence register.

## Contribute to the archive

Researchers, clinicians, engineers, patients, historians, and others with relevant experience can help improve the archive. Useful contributions include proposing a documented case, finding primary records, correcting an error, strengthening an evidence register, or pointing to relevant clinical and technical publications.

Please read [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md) before submitting a case. If this repository is hosted on GitHub, you can also use its Issues page to report a factual correction or suggest a source.
