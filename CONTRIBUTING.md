# Contributing

Contributions should add verifiable institutional memory, not a predetermined failure narrative.

## Firsthand accounts

First-person post-mortems may be authored only by founders, patients or surgeons with direct involvement in the case. Identify the author's standing and agree on what may be published, including names, quotations and personal details. Do not write in another person's voice or turn separate accounts into a fictional composite.

If you have not participated directly, submit a documented case dossier instead. Label recollections as testimony and distinguish them from records that independently establish events.

## Evidence and case proposals

For each material claim, provide a source that directly supports it, the source's publication date when verified, and a note about important limitations. Prefer regulatory records, trial registries, peer-reviewed studies, court records and dated statements from the organization. Use reporting to identify testimony and reconstruct context, while attributing claims to the reporter or speaker.

Submit a case proposal with:

1. The company, device, therapy, project or trial and the outcome being claimed.
2. A concise chronology with source links.
3. What is known, what is inferred, and what remains unknown.
4. Patient or user consequences supported by appropriate evidence.
5. Specific questions future teams should ask.

Do not send private medical records, private contact information or identifiable patient information without permission. Public case analysis is not individualized medical advice.

## Accuracy and response

For a significant claim about an identifiable person or organization, check the underlying evidence and, where practical, offer the subject a chance to correct factual errors before publication. Record whether a response was received, declined, unavailable or not sought. Silence is not evidence for an allegation.

## Style

Use concrete events, clear attributions and restrained analysis. Avoid unsupported motive claims, sensational titles, rankings and generic lessons. A precise exclusion is better than a weakly supported dossier.
