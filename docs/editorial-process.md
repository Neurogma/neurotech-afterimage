# Editorial process

## Admission

Before drafting a case, identify the correct entity and the exact outcome under review. Separate company closure, product discontinuation, acquisition, clinical endpoint failure, trial termination, device support and technology transfer.

A case may be admitted as a useful transition or support lesson without being called a failure. If the evidence does not establish the proposed outcome, record the candidate in docs/case-screening.md and do not write a failure narrative.

## Before integrating a change

Before publication, check:

1. **Identity and status:** Is this the correct company, product, trial or project? Is the outcome established?
2. **Chronology:** Do dates come from reliable records? Are acquisition, discontinuation, insolvency and support changes distinguished?
3. **Causality:** Does the text separate events from explanations? Are unknowns and plausible alternatives visible?
4. **Patient impact:** Do sources support the scope of the statement? Are sample and follow-up limits stated?
5. **Fairness:** Are serious claims attributed? Are motives and private conversations kept out unless documented?
6. **Transferable value:** Does each question future teams should ask arise from the case evidence?
7. **Authorship:** Is first-person writing genuinely from an eligible firsthand contributor?
8. **Response:** For a significant, potentially damaging claim, was a reasonable opportunity for correction offered where practical? Record only what was communicated and whether a response was received.

## Attribution and right of reply

Record whether a response was obtained, declined, unavailable or not sought. If a response is not sought, explain briefly when it matters to the evidence or fairness of the case. A subject's silence is never treated as proof.

## Revision

Corrections should identify the affected claim, the source that changed the conclusion and the revision date in the relevant dossier or validation record. Preserve a narrow conclusion if the evidence supports only a narrow conclusion.
