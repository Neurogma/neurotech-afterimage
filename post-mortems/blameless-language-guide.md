# Blameless-language guide

Blameless analysis makes decisions and consequences easier to examine. It does not hide responsibility for a documented decision.

## Keep these distinctions visible

- **Event and cause:** “The sponsor terminated the study after filing for bankruptcy” states an event and a documented reason. A separate claim about why the business failed needs separate evidence.
- **Error and character:** Describe a decision, its context and its effect. Do not infer that a person is careless, greedy or dishonest from an adverse outcome.
- **System and individual:** Identify constraints such as funding, supply, training, clinical workflow and regulation where records support them. Do not use “systemic” to erase a documented individual decision.
- **Criticism and allegation:** Attribute an allegation to the person or source making it and label whether it has been independently established.
- **Accountability and blame:** State who announced, approved or carried out a documented decision. Avoid unsupported claims about motive or intent.

## Examples

Prefer: “The company announced it would continue supporting existing users; later, users and clinicians reported that the former level of support was unavailable.”

Avoid: “The company abandoned all its patients.”

Prefer: “The randomized study did not meet its primary endpoint and stopped after a futility analysis.”

Avoid: “The treatment was a scam” or “the therapy was disproven.”

Use the stronger wording only when the evidence supports it, and give the source close to the claim.
