## Case identity and outcome

ADvance investigated deep brain stimulation (DBS) of the fornix in people with mild Alzheimer disease. The relevant outcome is not that DBS was proven ineffective. The pilot publication reported no significant difference between active stimulation and stimulation-off groups on the main cognitive measures at 12 months, and the completed follow-on ADvance II registry record did not show posted results in the registry record consulted. The first point is an inconclusive pilot result; the second is a results-availability limitation. [S-AD-01] [S-AD-02] [S-AD-03]

## What the public record shows

The public record contains two distinct evidence states. For the 42-participant ADvance pilot, the registry and publication report no significant 12-month difference in the main cognitive outcomes for the full cohort. The follow-on ADvance II record is marked completed and specifies 12-month change on iADRS as its primary efficacy endpoint, but the consulted registry record has no posted results. This is an inconclusive pilot result plus an outcome-reporting gap, not evidence that ADvance II was negative. [S-AD-01] [S-AD-02] [S-AD-03]

### What was tested

The ADvance pilot (NCT01608061) was a randomized, double-blind study with 42 participants: 21 assigned to active stimulation and 21 to stimulation off after DBS implantation. Its registry lists safety endpoints as primary, assessed acutely and through 12 months, and cognitive measures including ADAS-Cog13 and CDR-SB as secondary. The published paper describes ADAS-Cog13 and CDR-SB as primary cognitive outcomes; that difference in terminology between the registry’s outcome hierarchy and the publication should remain visible rather than being silently harmonized. [S-AD-01] [S-AD-02]

### Results and follow-on study

- **Pilot, 12 months.** The publication reported no significant between-group difference in cognitive outcomes for the overall cohort. The registry’s posted result fields list a mean ADAS-Cog13 change of 8.0 in both groups at 12 months (standard errors 2.2 active and 1.9 stimulation-off). The CDR-SB values were also close. These figures do not prove equivalence; sample size and interval estimates govern what can be excluded. [S-AD-01] [S-AD-02]
- **ADvance II (NCT03622905).** The official registry lists the study as completed and identifies change from baseline to 12 months on the Integrated Alzheimer’s Disease Rating Scale (iADRS) as the primary efficacy measure. No results were posted in the registry record available during this review. The absence leaves the registered primary outcome uninterpretable from that record alone; it does not mean the study found no effect, nor establish why results were not posted. [S-AD-03]

## What was assumed

The sources do not reveal all protocol-development deliberations, and this dossier does not attribute unverified assumptions to investigators. **Editorial inference:** completion status may be mistaken by readers for an interpretable result, and a non-significant pilot comparison may be mistaken for evidence of equivalence. The record supports neither shortcut: the follow-on registry result is absent, and the pilot's uncertainty does not establish a zero effect.

## What the evidence suggests future teams should do differently

**Editorial inference:** implanted neuromodulation in progressive neurodegenerative disease requires trials designed around slow change, placebo/control integrity, safety and a clinically meaningful endpoint. A pilot with no significant cognitive separation should constrain confidence but cannot settle the treatment question. Likewise, completion status is not a substitute for outcome reporting.

Publish the protocol, statistical analysis plan, participant flow and primary endpoint results regardless of direction; make registry and manuscript endpoint labels consistent or explain deviations. For follow-on studies, set in advance the minimum clinically important difference and show confidence intervals, not just p-values, so null and imprecise results can be distinguished.

## Consequences

The pilot supplies a randomized small-cohort comparison with no significant difference on stated cognitive measures. The follow-on study’s registry entry supplies the intended primary endpoint and study status, but not its result. No claim is made here that DBS has no role in Alzheimer disease overall, that every patient subgroup has the same response, or that ADvance II failed its primary endpoint.

## What remains unknown

- The final ADvance II iADRS result, analysis population, confidence interval and adverse-event findings from an authoritative results source.
- Why follow-on results were not posted in the registry in the registry record consulted.
- Whether full protocol/SAP changes altered the analysis plan over time.
- Whether subgroup hypotheses are replicated independently and adequately powered.

## Questions future teams should ask

- Which endpoints were primary in the registry, protocol, statistical plan and manuscript, and why do labels differ?
- What effect size and uncertainty would be clinically meaningful in a slow-progressing condition?
- Does “completed” refer to participant follow-up, database lock, analysis or publication?
- Who owns responsibility for reporting trial outcomes when sponsor or development strategy changes?
