## Case identity and outcome

ESTIVAL compared transcutaneous auricular vagus nerve stimulation (taVNS) with sham stimulation in erosive hand osteoarthritis. The report describes 142 randomized participants. At 12 weeks, the adjusted between-group difference in change in hand-pain VAS was −10.0 mm (95% CI −23.0 to 2.0; p=0.22), so the primary endpoint was not met. The abstract reports no serious adverse events. [S-EST-01]

## Evidence classification

**Documented fact:** The trial report/abstract supports the specific study design and outcome described above. **Editorial inference:** The institutional lesson is an interpretation, not a claim made directly by the study. **Unknown:** Protocol/registry reconciliation and/or full-text limits are stated below.

## What was built

The intervention used daily transcutaneous auricular vagus nerve stimulation over a 12-week period. The relevant clinical question was whether taVNS improved hand pain compared with sham stimulation in this defined population.

## What the public record shows

The publication abstract identifies the randomized, multicentre, double-blind, sham-controlled design and the primary endpoint. The authors mention a possible benefit in participants with greater synovial inflammation; the abstract alone does not establish that subgroup effect as confirmed. ClinicalTrials.gov lists the study as NCT04520516 and provides a record history with successive submissions. The existence of history changes does not itself establish a reporting discrepancy. [S-EST-01] [S-EST-02]

## What was assumed

This dossier does not infer that taVNS has no effect in every subgroup, that any registry changes were improper, or that the intervention class is ineffective for other indications.

## Consequences

ESTIVAL is useful for comparing a clearly reported primary result with subgroup interpretations and the public registry record. A full protocol-to-publication audit is needed before making claims about prespecification or reporting discrepancies.

## What remains unknown

This dossier draws on the abstract and registry history; it does not incorporate the publisher's full article, original protocol or statistical analysis plan. The prespecification and robustness of the synovial-inflammation subgroup require further verification.

## What the evidence suggests future teams should do differently

Make protocol and analysis-plan versions easy to retrieve, document registry changes with dates and rationales, and clearly distinguish primary outcomes from exploratory subgroup signals.

## Questions future teams should ask

- What do the original protocol and statistical analysis plan specify for the primary endpoint and subgroup analyses?
- Were outcome-measure changes made before or after unblinding?
- Does the full report provide adjusted estimates and multiplicity handling for subgroup findings?
- Do registry history and publication describe the same endpoint definitions and analysis populations?
