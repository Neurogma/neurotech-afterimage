## Case identity and outcome

ELECT-TDCS compared transcranial direct-current stimulation (tDCS), escitalopram and placebo in people with major depressive disorder. Its useful lesson is a compound one: **tDCS did not meet the prespecified noninferiority criterion versus escitalopram, but the trial reported superiority over placebo on the primary depression scale**. It would be inaccurate to call this a wholly negative trial or to claim that tDCS matched the active medication.

## What the public record shows

The public record includes a single-centre, randomized, double-blind, three-arm trial of 245 adults with unipolar depression, with week-10 change on the HDRS-17 as the primary outcome. The tDCS arm did not meet the prespecified noninferiority criterion against escitalopram, although both active treatments were statistically superior to placebo on the primary scale in this study. The distinction matters: failure to demonstrate noninferiority is not the same as showing no effect versus placebo. [S-ETDCS-01] [S-ETDCS-02]

### What was tested

The single-centre randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial enrolled 245 participants: 91 assigned to escitalopram, 94 to tDCS and 60 to placebo. The prespecified primary outcome was change in the 17-item Hamilton Depression Rating Scale (HDRS-17) at week 10. The noninferiority margin for tDCS compared with escitalopram was defined as −2.75 points under the study’s comparison framework. [S-ETDCS-01] [S-ETDCS-02]

### Primary results

- **Mean HDRS-17 score decrease by week 10.** Reported mean decreases were 11.3 points for escitalopram, 9.0 for tDCS and 5.8 for placebo. [S-ETDCS-02]
- **Noninferiority comparison.** The difference in score decrease for tDCS versus escitalopram was −2.3 points (95% CI −4.3 to −0.4). The study did not meet its prespecified noninferiority criterion; the reported P value for noninferiority was .69. “Not noninferior” does not establish that tDCS has no effect—it means the specified comparison did not demonstrate the required closeness to the active comparator. [S-ETDCS-02]
- **Placebo comparison.** tDCS was statistically superior to placebo on the primary scale, with a reported difference of 3.2 points (95% CI 0.7 to 5.5; P=.01); escitalopram was also superior to placebo. This finding supports an effect in the studied setting but does not show equivalence to medication or establish effectiveness for every tDCS protocol. [S-ETDCS-02]
- **Safety/tolerability.** The paper reported more skin redness, tinnitus and nervousness in the tDCS group, and two new-onset mania cases. These findings are relevant to monitoring and risk disclosure; they should be interpreted within the study’s population and sample size. [S-ETDCS-02]

## What was assumed

The paper specifies the comparison and margin, but the cited sources do not document every stakeholder's interpretation of the design. **Editorial inference:** a noninferiority test can be misread as a binary verdict on whether a treatment works. Here the criterion versus escitalopram was not met even though a placebo contrast favored tDCS. These are different hypotheses and should remain separate in trial communication.

## What the evidence suggests future teams should do differently

**Editorial inference:** noninferiority trials answer a different question from placebo-superiority trials. The noninferiority margin must be clinically justified in advance, and interpretation should show the point estimate and confidence interval against the margin. A failed noninferiority result cannot be converted into “no effect,” particularly when the same study reports a placebo comparison in the other direction.

Teams should report all planned contrasts together, disclose the analysis population and missing-data methods, provide a transparent rationale for the margin, and plan for adverse events that are not captured by efficacy endpoints. A single-center result should be replicated across settings and protocols before broad product claims are made.

## Consequences

The evidence supports that the tDCS arm improved more than placebo on the HDRS-17 primary measure in this trial, while the prespecified noninferiority comparison against escitalopram was not established. It does not support a claim that tDCS is as effective as escitalopram, nor a conclusion that the entire tDCS field has failed. The findings apply to this protocol, treatment schedule, participants and 10-week endpoint.

## What remains unknown

- How well the results generalize to other stimulation montages, intensities, treatment schedules and clinical populations.
- Whether the observed benefit persists beyond the trial’s principal endpoint without additional treatment.
- The precise balance of benefit, tolerability and risk in routine practice across larger and more diverse groups.

## Questions future teams should ask

- What is the clinically justified noninferiority margin, and would a patient consider it acceptable?
- Are point estimates and confidence intervals shown for every prespecified comparator?
- Does a failed noninferiority result get incorrectly described as proof of no effect?
- How are activation, mania and other neuropsychiatric adverse events monitored and reported?
