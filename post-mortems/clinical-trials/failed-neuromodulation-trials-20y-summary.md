# Selected neuromodulation trial outcomes, 2011–2017

The requested filename refers to “failed” trials, but the review uses more exact outcome language. It covers four selected randomized studies published between 2015 and 2017, registered between 2008 and 2011. It is not a comprehensive twenty-year review, not a reconstruction of Victor Pikov's subscriber whitepaper, and not a representative sample of neuromodulation research.

## Scope and method

The cases were selected because their registry and peer-reviewed records make it possible to identify an indication, primary endpoint and result. The review distinguishes (1) failure to meet a primary efficacy endpoint, (2) early stopping for futility, (3) a trial stopped for sponsor bankruptcy, and (4) later or secondary observations. “Trial result” does not mean “therapy class failure.”

For trial reports, the study publication is the source for endpoint results. ClinicalTrials.gov is used for the registered study identity and status. For some records the registry's dynamic page is difficult to inspect in a static browser, so the linked official record is supplied for readers to check its current history.

## Case identity and outcome

This is a selected-trial review of four registered randomized studies, not one device or sponsor dossier. The documented outcomes range from negative primary endpoint results to futility stopping; one trial's longer-term safety follow-up continued after its randomized efficacy phase. [S-TR-01] [S-TR-03] [S-TR-05] [S-TR-07]

## What the public record shows

The public records document four selected studies with distinct outcomes: two depression DBS trials that did not show a significant primary response difference from sham, one heart-failure VNS study that did not show improvement on its primary remodeling endpoint, and one heart-failure VNS trial stopped for futility without a significant primary event benefit. They do not support a claim that neuromodulation as a field failed. [S-TR-02] [S-TR-04] [S-TR-06] [S-TR-08]

## Trial summaries

This section compares four deliberately selected cases—two sham-controlled DBS trials for treatment-resistant depression and two vagus-nerve-stimulation studies in heart failure. They illustrate different endpoints and reasons an intervention may not demonstrate the intended primary benefit: two trials did not establish superiority over sham, one did not improve its primary cardiac-remodelling endpoint, and one stopped after an interim futility analysis without showing a primary event benefit. These trials are illustrative, not a representative census of neuromodulation research, and their outcomes cannot be generalized to the entire field. [S-TR-01] [S-TR-02] [S-TR-03] [S-TR-04] [S-TR-05] [S-TR-06] [S-TR-07] [S-TR-08]

### BROADEN: subcallosal cingulate DBS for treatment-resistant depression

- **Identity:** Subcallosal cingulate deep brain stimulation for treatment-resistant depression, ClinicalTrials.gov NCT00617162.
- **Population and intervention:** Adults with chronic treatment-resistant major depression; bilateral DBS of subcallosal cingulate white matter.
- **Design:** Planned 201-person, multisite, randomized double-blind sham-controlled study with six blinded months followed by open-label treatment. A futility analysis was conducted after about half the planned sample had reached the blinded phase.
- **Primary endpoint:** Response, defined as at least a 40% reduction in depression severity, averaged over months 4–6 of the double-blind phase.
- **Result:** 90 participants were randomized (60 active, 30 sham). Response occurred in 12/60 (20%) active participants and 5/30 (17%) sham participants; the difference was not statistically significant. The study was stopped after the futility analysis. The publication reported 40 serious adverse events in 28 participants; eight were judged related to the device or surgery.
- **Interpretation limit:** The result is evidence that this trial did not establish superiority over sham at its prespecified time point. It does not establish that DBS cannot help any subgroup or that another target/design will have the same result. Later open-label observations are not equivalent to the blinded comparison.

Sources: [S-TR-01] [S-TR-02]

### RECLAIM: ventral capsule / ventral striatum DBS for treatment-resistant depression

- **Identity:** Reclaim Deep Brain Stimulation Clinical Study for Treatment-Resistant Depression, ClinicalTrials.gov NCT00837486.
- **Population and intervention:** Adults with chronic treatment-resistant depression; bilateral DBS of the ventral capsule / ventral striatum.
- **Design:** Multicenter randomized sham-controlled phase lasting 16 weeks, followed by open-label continuation. Thirty patients entered the randomized phase.
- **Primary endpoint:** Response, defined as at least a 50% improvement on the Montgomery–Åsberg Depression Rating Scale from baseline.
- **Result:** At week 16, response was 3/15 (20%) in the active group and 2/14 (14.3%) in the sham group; no significant group difference was found. Open-label response rates were reported at later time points, but those observations do not replace the sham comparison.
- **Interpretation limit:** The small sample limits precision. The absence of a significant difference in this trial is not proof that every patient or protocol will have no benefit. The endpoint and target differed from BROADEN's.

Sources: [S-TR-03] [S-TR-04]

### NECTAR-HF: cervical VNS for heart failure

- **Identity:** Neural Cardiac Therapy for Heart Failure Study, ClinicalTrials.gov NCT01385176.
- **Population and intervention:** Patients with symptomatic systolic heart failure received right cervical vagal nerve stimulation or inactive stimulation in a 2:1 randomized phase.
- **Design:** Ninety-six patients randomized for six months; after the randomized phase stimulation was activated for all participants. The study remained active but not recruiting in the ClinicalTrials.gov record reviewed, because longer-term follow-up continued.
- **Primary efficacy endpoint:** Change in left ventricular end-systolic dimension (LVESD) at six months. The later 18-month report also specifies an 18-month all-cause mortality safety endpoint.
- **Result:** The six-month randomized phase did not show improvement in LV remodeling. The 18-month report found a favorable safety profile but no improvement in efficacy endpoints. Some secondary quality-of-life or functional measures were reported as improved in analyses, but they do not overturn the primary efficacy result.
- **Interpretation limit:** The study's endpoint result does not establish that all VNS approaches for heart failure are ineffective. Participants crossed to active stimulation after six months, limiting controlled long-term efficacy comparison.

Sources: [S-TR-05] [S-TR-06]

### INOVATE-HF: CardioFit VNS for heart failure

- **Identity:** INcrease Of VAgal TonE in CHF, ClinicalTrials.gov NCT01303718.
- **Population and intervention:** Patients with chronic heart failure, NYHA class III symptoms and left-ventricular ejection fraction at or below 40%; CardioFit VNS plus medical therapy versus medical therapy.
- **Design:** Open-label randomized phase 3 study with an active-to-control allocation ratio of 3:2. The publication reports 707 randomized participants at 85 centers. It stopped in December 2015 on the independent monitoring board's recommendation after a planned interim analysis for futility.
- **Primary efficacy endpoint:** Composite of death from any cause or a first event attributed to worsening heart failure.
- **Result:** The endpoint occurred in 132/436 (30.3%) of VNS participants and 70/271 (25.8%) of controls; hazard ratio 1.14 (95% CI 0.86–1.53), p=0.37. The study did not show a reduction in death or heart-failure events. Quality of life, NYHA class and six-minute walk distance favored VNS in secondary analyses.
- **Interpretation limit:** The study's open-label design can affect subjective secondary outcomes. The secondary findings do not establish benefit on the primary event endpoint, and the negative endpoint does not establish a class-wide result for all autonomic or VNS interventions.

Sources: [S-TR-07] [S-TR-08]

## What the selected cases establish

**Documented fact:** BROADEN and RECLAIM did not show a significant active-versus-sham difference on their primary depression response outcomes. INOVATE-HF stopped for futility after its event-driven interim review and did not meet its primary efficacy endpoint. NECTAR-HF did not show benefit on its six-month cardiac-remodeling efficacy endpoint, while its safety and longer-term follow-up continued. [S-TR-02] [S-TR-04] [S-TR-06] [S-TR-08]

**Editorial inference:** The set demonstrates why endpoint choice, blinding, follow-up design, sample size, and the reason for stopping must be examined separately. It cannot identify a general cause of neuromodulation-trial failure or predict the result of a different therapy.

## What remains unknown

- A comprehensive search of all neuromodulation trials over the last twenty years.
- How the four selected trial results compare with every successful, inconclusive or unpublished study in the same indications.
- Whether later studies changed targeting, stimulation parameters or patient selection enough to test a distinct hypothesis.
- The cases, investment figures, scores and classifications in Victor Pikov's whitepaper, which was described by its publisher as subscriber-only and was not accessed for this release. [S-TR-09]

## What the evidence suggests future teams should do differently

Prespecify the clinically meaningful primary endpoint, estimate the sample needed to test it, and decide how futility, safety and secondary findings will be reported before enrollment. Make those distinctions visible in public reporting. This is a design recommendation inferred from the selected records, not a causal conclusion about why each sponsor's study did not meet its goal.

## Questions future teams should ask

- Does the registered primary endpoint reflect a benefit that patients and clinicians recognize as meaningful?
- Are secondary findings clearly distinguished from the primary result?
- If the study stops early, does the public record state whether the reason was futility, safety, recruitment, funding or administration?
- What follow-up can still be interpreted after a sham-controlled phase ends?
- Are results reported against the original protocol and statistical analysis plan?
