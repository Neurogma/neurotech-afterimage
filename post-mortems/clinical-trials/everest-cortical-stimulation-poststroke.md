## Case identity and outcome

EVEREST was a pivotal randomized trial of epidural motor-cortex stimulation delivered during upper-limb rehabilitation for people with residual motor impairment after ischemic stroke. The prespecified primary efficacy endpoint was not met. At the four-week post-treatment assessment, 30.8% of evaluable investigational participants and 29.1% of evaluable controls met the composite threshold for clinically meaningful improvement. The absolute difference was 1.7 percentage points, far below the 20-point difference specified in the endpoint. [S-EVR-01] [S-EVR-02]

The published safety/design analysis reports 373 people consented, 164 randomized (104 investigational, 60 control), and 94 participants received an implant. Controls received the same rehabilitation schedule without device implantation; this was not a sham-craniotomy control. The number 318 and the claim that participants in both groups underwent craniotomy are not supported by the sources used for this dossier. [S-EVR-02]

## Evidence classification

**Documented fact:** The primary endpoint result and trial flow are supported by the company’s SEC-filed announcement and a peer-reviewed analysis. **Editorial inference:** The trial illustrates the challenge of estimating the incremental effect of an invasive adjunct when both groups receive intensive rehabilitation. **Unknown:** It is not possible from the cited sources to identify a validated subgroup for whom stimulation would have reliably improved outcomes.

## What was built

The investigational system delivered subthreshold epidural electrical stimulation over motor cortex during structured upper-limb rehabilitation. The control group received rehabilitation without implantation. The design therefore estimated the additional effect of stimulation relative to rehabilitation alone, not relative to a matched sham surgical procedure. [S-EVR-02]

## What the public record shows

The study-design publication describes 21 centres and a 2:1 randomization, with up to six weeks of therapy and follow-up. The later analysis states that the trial failed to attain the primary endpoint at four weeks. It also discusses post-hoc functional differences at 24 weeks, but cautions that these findings must be interpreted in light of the absence of a sham-surgery group and the possibility of differential attention to the investigational group. Those later observations do not reverse the prespecified primary result. [S-EVR-02]

The company announced the primary endpoint result on 22 January 2008. Its release reported an investigational/control responder difference of 1.7 percentage points and a one-sided p value of 0.41. As a company announcement, it is a primary record of the announced result, while the peer-reviewed analysis provides more detail on the trial flow and design limitations. [S-EVR-01] [S-EVR-02]

## What was assumed

This case does not establish that all cortical stimulation is ineffective, that the rehabilitation received by controls was unexpectedly strong, or that a biomarker-defined subgroup would have benefited. The control group's performance is an observed outcome, not evidence of an identified causal mechanism for the primary result.

## Consequences

The investigational arm exposed participants to an invasive implant procedure. The source does not support saying that all 164 randomized participants underwent craniotomy: the peer-reviewed report records 94 implants, while the control group did not receive an implant. In an invasive adjunct trial, the evidentiary threshold should reflect not only statistical significance but also the incremental clinical benefit needed to justify procedural burden and risk. [S-EVR-02]

## What remains unknown

The reviewed evidence does not establish a prospectively validated response biomarker, nor whether any post-hoc subgroup would replicate in a new trial. A detailed protocol/SAP audit and individual-participant-data review were not undertaken for this dossier.

## What the evidence suggests future teams should do differently

Predefine a clinically meaningful between-group effect and avoid interpreting secondary or post-hoc signals as substitutes for a failed primary endpoint. Where feasible and ethically appropriate, consider how control design and blinded outcome assessment address expectations and differential attention. Publish participant flow, adverse events and long-term outcomes in enough detail to allow independent appraisal.

## Questions future teams should ask

- What incremental benefit is large enough to justify an invasive adjunct to intensive rehabilitation?
- How will trial design distinguish stimulation effects from surgical expectations and differential attention?
- Are any subgroup or biomarker analyses prospectively specified, adequately powered and independently replicated?
- What is the plan for device removal, follow-up and participant support when an investigational system is discontinued?
