## Case identity and outcome

ANTHEM-HFrEF (NCT03425422) evaluated vagal nerve stimulation in patients with heart failure with reduced ejection fraction. The 2026 report states that the sponsor stopped enrollment after 532 participants had been randomized for reasons described as unrelated to futility or efficacy. The primary efficacy endpoint was not met, and the report characterized the result as underpowered and inconclusive. This is an extension to the repository's existing selected neuromodulation-trial synthesis, not an independent new case for portfolio counting. [S-ANT-01] [S-ANT-02]

## Evidence classification

**Documented fact:** The trial report/abstract supports the specific study design and outcome described above. **Editorial inference:** The institutional lesson is an interpretation, not a claim made directly by the study. **Unknown:** Protocol/registry reconciliation and/or full-text limits are stated below.

## What was built

The trial studied vagal nerve stimulation as an adjunctive intervention in patients with heart failure with reduced ejection fraction. The relevant institutional event combines an enrollment stop with a primary efficacy result that did not establish benefit.

## What the public record shows

The 2026 journal report describes sponsor-stopped enrollment, the stated reason as unrelated to futility or efficacy, and the unmet primary efficacy endpoint. It also reports that the Data Monitoring Committee had recommended continuation unchanged at the preceding interim analysis. These details should be kept distinct: the enrollment stop should not be recast as a futility decision. [S-ANT-01]

The study is registered as NCT03425422. Registry details should be checked against the final publication and protocol versions during any future full audit. [S-ANT-02]

## What was assumed

This dossier does not infer sponsor motive beyond the explanation reported in the publication, nor does it infer that the trial was stopped because the intervention was ineffective. The primary endpoint result does not establish that all vagal nerve stimulation approaches are ineffective for heart failure.

## Consequences

The case provides a useful governance lesson: reasons for stopping enrollment, interim monitoring recommendations, final efficacy results, and statistical power are separate facts and should be documented separately. It may materially extend the existing synthesis without duplicating it as a separate portfolio-level event.

## What remains unknown

This draft is based on the publisher's article page/abstract and registry identification; a complete protocol, statistical analysis plan, supplement and registry-history reconciliation has not been performed here. The full details of the sponsor's operational rationale beyond the reported statement remain unknown.

## What the evidence suggests future teams should do differently

Predefine and publicly report the distinction between safety/futility recommendations and sponsor operational decisions; explain the effect of enrollment stopping on statistical power and interpret the final estimate accordingly.

## Questions future teams should ask

- What reason for stopping is documented in the final paper, protocol and sponsor disclosures?
- What did the Data Monitoring Committee recommend, and when?
- How did the actual sample size affect precision and power for the primary endpoint?
- Which facts support a clinical interpretation, and which describe governance or sponsor operations?
