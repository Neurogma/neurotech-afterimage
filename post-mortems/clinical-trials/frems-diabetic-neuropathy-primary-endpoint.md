## Case identity and outcome

Bosi et al. reported a double-blind, randomized, multicentre, placebo-controlled trial of frequency-modulated electromagnetic neural stimulation (FREMS) for symptomatic diabetic neuropathy. The study did not demonstrate a between-group difference in its primary nerve-conduction-velocity endpoint. This is a study-specific result, not proof that every possible symptom-related effect or every protocol is ineffective. [S-FREMS-01]

## Evidence classification

**Documented fact:** The trial report/abstract supports the specific study design and outcome described above. **Editorial inference:** The institutional lesson is an interpretation, not a claim made directly by the study. **Unknown:** Protocol/registry reconciliation and/or full-text limits are stated below.

## What was built

FREMS is a non-invasive electromagnetic stimulation intervention investigated for symptomatic diabetic neuropathy. The trial enrolled 110 participants and included repeated treatment cycles and long-term follow-up.

## What the public record shows

The paper specifies nerve conduction velocities of the deep peroneal, tibial and sural nerves as the primary endpoint. The intention-to-treat analysis did not find significant differences between FREMS and placebo on these measures. The paper also reports pain reductions immediately after treatment sessions that were no longer measurable three months later. These observations should be kept distinct from the primary endpoint. [S-FREMS-01]

## What was assumed

No inference is made that the intervention has no effect in every population, that all secondary findings are invalid, or that the product was discontinued. The paper supports a trial-specific endpoint conclusion only.

## Consequences

The case illustrates the importance of preserving prespecified endpoints, distinguishing physiological outcomes from symptom measures, and separating immediate response from durability at later follow-up.

## What remains unknown

The complete registry amendment history and original protocol/statistical analysis plan were not exhaustively audited in this repository update. Current commercial availability and long-term device support are outside the central claim and remain unverified here.

## What the evidence suggests future teams should do differently

Publish and maintain accessible protocol and analysis-plan versions, report primary and secondary outcomes separately, and specify follow-up windows that distinguish immediate response from durable clinical benefit.

## Questions future teams should ask

- Was the primary endpoint fixed before enrollment and analysis?
- Are immediate and durable symptom effects reported separately?
- Which secondary measures were prespecified, and how should they be interpreted relative to the primary result?
- Is the device's current market/support status documented independently of the clinical paper?
