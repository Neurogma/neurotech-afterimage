# Nuvectra / Algovita: bankruptcy and continuity of care

This dossier separates the company's insolvency, the legal sale of assets, termination of a sponsored trial, and the experience of individual clinicians. The evidence does not establish that Algovita failed clinically.

## Case identity and outcome

Nuvectra's Algovita was an implanted spinal-cord stimulation system indicated by the FDA as an aid in managing chronic intractable pain of the trunk or limbs. FDA approved its premarket approval application in November 2015. [S-NV-01]

**Documented fact:** Nuvectra filed for Chapter 11 protection on 2019-11-12. In its announcement, the company said it had taken on substantial debt to fund innovation and commercialization, and said it was exploring options for the company and its assets. [S-NV-02]

**Documented fact:** Nuvectra initially stated that it would maintain specialists to support existing Algovita patients and clinicians. Its physician FAQ also stated that support for future implants was being suspended and recommended stopping new implantations and studies while the company determined a path forward. [S-NV-02] [S-NV-03]

**Unknown:** The record reviewed does not establish how consistently existing-user support continued across sites or after Nuvectra's operations were wound down.

## What was built and tested

Algovita delivered spinal-cord stimulation for chronic intractable trunk and limb pain. The product had FDA approval, and Nuvectra also sponsored the Algovita Hi-Fi Study, registered as NCT03622866. That study record was later marked terminated with the reason “Sponsor bankruptcy.” This registry status describes the study's administrative outcome; it is not a negative efficacy result. [S-NV-01] [S-NV-04]

**Attributed testimony:** Bux told Nature that he learned patients would no longer have coverage and service shortly after the filing. That account conflicts with Nuvectra's contemporaneous public commitment to support existing users. The sources cited do not resolve the exact transition date or whether the company statement was met at every site. [S-NV-03] [S-NV-06]

**Documented fact:** A court-approved asset sale is not, by itself, evidence that the buyer assumed clinical support obligations. The sale document identifies specified assets and an acquired contract; it does not identify an Algovita patient-service commitment. [S-NV-05]

## Cost of the assumption

For patients, interruption of support for an implanted stimulator can leave clinicians with fewer options for programming, troubleshooting and replacement planning. In Nature's reporting, Bux said some patients could move to similar devices from established manufacturers, but described the burden of replacement surgery, recovery and cost. These are attributed observations from his practice, not measured outcomes for all recipients. [S-NV-06]

For research, the Hi-Fi Study ended because its sponsor entered bankruptcy. The registry does not say that it stopped for futility, safety or lack of efficacy. [S-NV-04]

## What remains unknown

- The total number of Algovita recipients affected by support changes.
- Whether the support commitments in the November 2019 announcement were fulfilled consistently.
- Whether Cirtec or another entity accepted ongoing duties for existing users.
- Whether later Algovita devices or programs were maintained, transferred or discontinued.
- The clinical result that the Hi-Fi Study might have produced if it had continued.

No direct right-of-reply request was made. Company statements and attributed clinical testimony are kept distinct. The report does not allege misconduct.

## What the evidence suggests future teams should do differently

Before implanting a device at scale, define and fund a support plan that can survive insolvency. The Nuvectra record supports asking whether continuity obligations are operationally explicit: its public commitment, suspension of future implant support, later court-supervised sale and the clinician's account do not together show who ultimately owned the patient-support function.

## Questions future teams should ask

- Are existing patients and clinical sites covered by a funded support plan during restructuring?
- Does an asset purchase expressly transfer software, parts, records, training and support obligations?
- How will clinicians learn that service levels have changed, and who will coordinate replacement care?
- Are trial records and sponsor responsibilities preserved if the company enters bankruptcy?
