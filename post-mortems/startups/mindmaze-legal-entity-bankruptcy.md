## Case identity and outcome

This dossier concerns two named Swiss companies: MindMaze Group SA (UID CHE-477.979.617) and MindMaze SA (UID CHE-261.160.131). The Vaud cantonal commercial-register publication states that a Lausanne District Court decision on 20 April 2026 declared both companies bankrupt and changed their names to forms ending “en liquidation.” The event is a consequential legal-entity outcome. It must not be summarized as “all MindMaze technology closed”: MindMaze Therapeutics Holding SA is a distinct listed business that continued to publish product and corporate activity during 2026. [S-MM-01] [S-MM-02] [S-MM-03]

## What the public record shows

The official Vaud commercial-register notice records bankruptcy effective 20 April 2026 for MindMaze Group SA and MindMaze SA, identifying each entity separately. Separately, MindMaze Therapeutics Holding SA published a product announcement in September 2026. These records establish that two named entities entered bankruptcy while a distinct business continued public product activity; they do not establish a full transfer of intellectual property, employees, contracts, regulatory responsibilities or patient-support obligations between them. [S-MM-01] [S-MM-02] [S-MM-03]

### What was built

The broader MindMaze ecosystem has developed neurorehabilitation technologies, including products for stroke rehabilitation and related software-enabled therapy. Public product pages associated with MindMaze Therapeutics describe a separate present-day product and corporate operation. This context is relevant for disambiguation only; it does not show which specific assets or responsibilities belonged to the bankrupt entities. [S-MM-02] [S-MM-03]

### Chronology and entity distinction

- **20 April 2026 — court decision.** The official Vaud cantonal register’s publication states that the Lausanne District Court revoked definitive concordat stays and declared MindMaze Group SA and MindMaze SA bankrupt effective at 09:00 on that date. The register lists the legal identifiers and their subsequent liquidation names. [S-MM-01]
- **28 April 2026 — register notice.** The official notice was published on that date. This is a legal-status statement about the named entities, not about every business using a related name, all staff, all patents, or every product. [S-MM-01]
- **2026 — distinct listed business remains active publicly.** MindMaze Therapeutics Holding SA’s website describes current activity, and a September 2026 product announcement discusses MindMotion Lite as a home-based neurorehabilitation offering. These sources establish current public activity by the separate listed business; they do not explain the full intercompany asset map. [S-MM-02] [S-MM-03]

## What was assumed

The review contains no internal board materials from the affected entities, so it cannot say what executives or customers believed about the corporate structure. **Editorial inference:** a common but unsafe assumption is that the bankruptcy of a company with a familiar product name means the whole technology ecosystem has failed—or, conversely, that activity by a related listed company proves that all legacy obligations transferred. Neither shortcut is established by the official notice and successor-company announcements reviewed here.

## What the evidence suggests future teams should do differently

**Editorial inference:** neurotechnology groups often operate through holding companies, operating subsidiaries, intellectual-property owners, device manufacturers and clinical distributors. A bankruptcy notice for one legal entity should prompt a trace of ownership and duties—not a conclusion about an entire technology ecosystem.

Product-risk planning should document which entity owns source code and patents, which entity holds regulatory registrations, who manufactures and services deployed systems, and what happens to clinical data and patient support when a particular subsidiary enters insolvency. For a buyer or investor, public continuity of a brand or successor product is not enough to establish that legacy liabilities and support commitments were assumed.

## Consequences

The official register establishes bankruptcy of two specific legal entities. Publicly available corporate material separately establishes that MindMaze Therapeutics continued product activity. The cited record does not establish that the bankrupt entities' assets, employees, regulatory responsibilities, contracts or patient-support obligations all moved to MindMaze Therapeutics, nor that every MindMaze-branded product stopped functioning or became unsupported.

## What remains unknown

- The final inventory of assets, patents, code, contracts and liabilities held by each bankrupt entity.
- Whether particular intellectual property or support obligations were transferred, and under what legal instrument.
- The operational relationship, if any, between the bankrupt entities and MindMaze Therapeutics Holding SA at the relevant dates.
- The effect, if any, on clinicians or institutions using products associated historically with the MindMaze name.

## Questions future teams should ask

- Which exact legal entity is contracting with the institution and named as product manufacturer?
- Is a parent company guarantee in place for servicing, cybersecurity, data access and replacement parts?
- Can administrators identify where source code, device history files and essential expertise reside?
- Does a public announcement from a related brand actually establish transfer of legacy duties?
