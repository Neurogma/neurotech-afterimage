## Case identity and outcome

NeuroVista developed an implantable system intended to warn people with drug-resistant focal epilepsy of periods when seizures were more likely. The outcome documented here is **development ending after a trial was terminated for sponsor restructuring**, not proof that the forecasting concept was invalid or that every company asset disappeared on a particular date.

**Documented fact:** ClinicalTrials.gov records the prospective feasibility study NCT01043406 as terminated, with the recorded reason “Sponsor restructuring.” The record’s final update is dated 24 October 2012, and the registry does not contain posted results. That absence is a property of the registry record, not proof that the experiment was never published. [S-NVS-01] [S-NVS-02]

## What the public record shows

The public record shows a difference between trial-registry reporting and the scientific literature. ClinicalTrials.gov marks the Seizure Advisory System Feasibility Study (NCT01043406) as terminated for “sponsor restructuring” and has no results posted in the registry; the peer-reviewed first-in-human study nevertheless reports feasibility and safety data from 15 implanted participants. The study reported that algorithm performance criteria were met in 11 of 15 participants, while clinical effectiveness measures did not change significantly over four months. The termination entry does not itself establish the full corporate fate of NeuroVista or every related asset. [S-NVS-01] [S-NVS-02] [S-NVS-03] [S-NVS-04]

### What was built

The implant measured intracranial electrical activity and communicated a forecast state through an external warning device. The study evaluated safety, algorithm performance and clinical measures in adults with drug-resistant focal seizures. This was a feasibility-stage system, not evidence that a broadly deployable consumer product or reimbursed standard of care had been established. [S-NVS-02]

### Chronology and results

- **24 March 2010–21 June 2011 — enrollment window reported in the publication.** Fifteen people were implanted across three Melbourne centres. The study’s prespecified primary endpoint was adverse events through four months; algorithm performance and clinical effectiveness were secondary measures. [S-NVS-02]
- **Four-month safety assessment.** Eleven participants experienced device-related adverse events within the first four months; two events were serious (device migration and seroma). Two further serious events in year one were reported as infection and a site reaction. The paper also reported removal of one device before enough training data could be collected. These figures describe this small feasibility cohort and should not be generalized to other implants. [S-NVS-02]
- **Forecast performance.** Algorithms met the study’s enabling criteria in 11 of 15 participants. Reported sensitivity for high-likelihood warnings ranged from 65% to 100%; three algorithms did not satisfy the performance criteria. The wide range and participant-level variation are central to interpretation. [S-NVS-02]
- **Clinical measures.** The authors reported no significant change in clinical effectiveness measures between baseline and four months. That is not equivalent to a powered demonstration of no patient benefit; the sample was small and this was a feasibility study. [S-NVS-02]
- **October 2012 — sponsor restructuring.** The registry says the trial was terminated for that reason. It does not give a detailed account of the restructuring or identify the precise corporate/legal fate of every asset. [S-NVS-01]
- **Later retrospective account.** A 2019 retrospective article describes a difficult funding environment and a proposed strategic financing route for a larger pivotal study. Those explanations are attributable to the article’s author and are not an independently established causal finding. A 2026 review of seizure forecasting describes the system as not having progressed to further commercialization and discusses financing, reimbursement and regulatory barriers alongside the clinical evidence. [S-NVS-03] [S-NVS-04]

## What was assumed

The sources cited do not record a complete internal decision log, so no belief is attributed to NeuroVista's team. **Editorial inference:** the development path required forecasting performance in individual users, acceptable implant risk, evidence of clinical utility and funding for a pivotal study to converge. Treating performance on one dimension as sufficient for the next would be a risky assumption; the public record shows variation in algorithm performance and non-trivial device-related events, while the financing pathway remains partly described only retrospectively.

## What the evidence suggests future teams should do differently

**Editorial inference:** NeuroVista illustrates a translation bottleneck across several linked workstreams: implant safety, individualized algorithm performance, meaningful clinical benefit, regulatory strategy and the capital required for a pivotal study. The source record does not identify one factor as the sole cause of the development outcome.

Future teams should budget and gate separately for (1) technical forecast accuracy, (2) patient-facing utility, (3) implant and explant burden, (4) prospective clinical endpoints, (5) reimbursement and deployment, and (6) the financing runway needed to reach a decision-grade trial. A system that produces useful forecasts for some participants may still fail to establish a commercially or clinically sustainable pathway. Conversely, financing or organizational interruption does not invalidate every scientific observation already published.

## Consequences

The publication supports that some participants experienced forecast warnings and that device-related adverse events occurred. It does not establish the scale of any lasting harm, the later experience of every participant, or whether the technology was transferred into another supported product. Do not convert the trial’s 15 participants into a population-level estimate.

## What remains unknown

- The exact date, legal form and jurisdiction of any final corporate dissolution, if one occurred.
- Whether all relevant hardware, algorithms, data and intellectual property were transferred to another organization.
- Whether the proposed pivotal-study financing ever became executable, and the precise financial reasons it did or did not proceed.
- Long-term participant outcomes beyond what the cited studies report.
- Why registry results were not posted despite publication of trial results in a peer-reviewed paper.

## Questions future teams should ask

- What evidence would justify proceeding from a feasibility algorithm to a pivotal clinical endpoint?
- Are individualized performance thresholds reported participant by participant, including exclusions and incomplete training data?
- Who is responsible for explant, follow-up and data retention if sponsorship ends mid-study?
- Is the financing plan sufficient to fund the next study and the support obligations created by the current one?
