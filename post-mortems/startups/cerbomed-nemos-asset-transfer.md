## Case identity and outcome

Cerbomed GmbH developed NEMOS, a handheld transcutaneous auricular vagus-nerve stimulation (taVNS) system investigated for epilepsy. In April 2017, a patient-facing epilepsy publication reported that Cerbomed had informed it that the company was ending operations because it had not developed a sustainably profitable business model. The same report said the company was, at that time, still accepting email enquiries about support and warranty. A later peer-reviewed review states that the NEMOS system was taken over by tVNS Technologies GmbH in October 2018. [S-CERB-01] [S-CERB-02]

The supported outcome is therefore an operational shutdown followed by a reported product/technology transfer—not proof that NEMOS was abandoned permanently or that all users were left unsupported. The available evidence is insufficient to map every asset, contract, clinical record or legacy support obligation across the transition.

## Evidence classification

**Documented fact:** The 2017 source reports a company statement about ending operations; a 2020 review states NEMOS was taken over by tVNS Technologies in 2018. **Attributed testimony:** The explanation about the unsustainable business model is Cerbomed's own reported statement. **Editorial inference:** The case highlights the difference between a company ending operations and a product line passing to another organization. **Unknown:** The exact legal scope of the transfer and continuity of service for individual users.

## What was built

NEMOS was a non-invasive system using auricular stimulation to target the auricular branch of the vagus nerve. A peer-reviewed review describes a handheld pulse generator and daily use of four hours, and notes that the system held European CE marking. The device's technical or clinical merits are not adjudicated in this corporate-transition dossier. [S-CERB-02]

## What the public record shows

The April 2017 notice reported that Cerbomed had decided to stop all operational activities after failing to establish a sustainable profitable business model. At the time of the notice, the company reportedly invited patients to contact it by email with device-support and warranty questions. That contemporaneous statement does not establish how long support remained available after the notice. [S-CERB-01]

A later review article states that NEMOS was taken over by tVNS Technologies GmbH in October 2018. This indicates a successor pathway for the product, but the source cited does not provide a complete transaction agreement or specify transfer of every service function, warranty or participant obligation. [S-CERB-02]

## What was assumed

This dossier does not claim that NEMOS was clinically ineffective, that low adherence or lack of reimbursement caused the shutdown, or that patients universally lost access to consumables or calibration. Those claims were not established by the cited sources. Nor does a reported takeover alone prove complete continuity of patient support.

## Consequences

When a company ceases operations, people using its medical technology may need to know who can maintain, repair or replace equipment, respond to safety questions and honour warranties. A later product takeover can reduce disruption, but users and clinicians need an explicit statement of what the successor does—and does not—assume.

## What remains unknown

The transaction's legal scope, continuing availability of NEMOS hardware and consumables, migration of warranties and customer records, and support arrangements for users during the transition are not established by the cited sources. The dossier does not assess clinical efficacy beyond identifying the product's intended context.

## What the evidence suggests future teams should do differently

Separate operating-company continuity from product continuity in public communications. When assets or products are transferred, publish a named support contact, a compatibility and supply statement, an effective date, and a clear explanation of which warranties, records and clinical-support duties are transferred. Preserve a documented fallback for users if a buyer does not assume those functions.

## Questions future teams should ask

- Which legal entity owns the device, software, technical documentation and post-market obligations after a transfer?
- What was the last date on which the original manufacturer accepted support or warranty requests?
- Which support duties did the successor explicitly assume, and which remained with the insolvent or dissolved company?
- Can users and clinicians still obtain consumables, repair, safety information and access to device-history records?
