## Case identity and outcome

Halo Sport was a consumer-facing headset using transcranial direct-current stimulation (tDCS) marketed to athletes for motor-training sessions. Halo’s current support materials explicitly identify Halo Sport as a discontinued headset and distinguish it from a later Halo headset aimed at prefrontal stimulation and mental-wellbeing/cognitive use. Flow Neuroscience announced in February 2021 that it acquired assets of Halo Neuroscience, including technology and intellectual property. The documented outcome is **product discontinuation during an asset and product transition**, not proof that every Halo-branded product, app or consumable disappeared. [S-HALO-01] [S-HALO-02] [S-HALO-03]

## What the public record shows

The record supports a bounded conclusion about the original Halo Sport headset. Halo's support page explicitly identifies Halo Sport as discontinued, and Flow Neuroscience announced an acquisition of Halo assets including technology and patents in 2021. Halo's current website says headsets are no longer available to purchase while still describing replacement pads for existing users. Thus the old hardware's sales status changed and some continuity is described for consumables, but the sources do not prove that every software, account, warranty or servicing path remains available—or that all support has ended. [S-HALO-01] [S-HALO-02] [S-HALO-03]

### What was built

Halo Sport combined a headset with electrode-contact accessories (“primers”) intended to deliver stimulation during athletic practice. The current support page warns users not to conflate it with the later Halo product, whose stated target and intended use differ. A product name continuing under the same or related brand does not establish that the hardware, protocol, software support or performance claims are interchangeable. [S-HALO-01]

### Chronology and support boundary

- **5 February 2021 — asset acquisition announcement.** Flow Neuroscience announced that it had acquired assets of Halo Neuroscience, naming technology and patents and describing its intention to build on the research for mental-health tDCS. The announcement supports an asset transaction and stated strategic intent; it does not by itself prove transfer of every customer contract, warranty, app function or support obligation. [S-HALO-02]
- **Later support materials — Halo Sport identified as discontinued.** The Halo help centre explains the difference between the discontinued Halo Sport and the newer Halo headset. It does not give a complete dated support matrix for each Sport generation or software version. [S-HALO-01]
- **Current website status.** Halo’s official site says headsets are no longer available for purchase / are sold out, while describing replacement pads for existing users as available through the Halo app and referring to Flow Treatment in the United States. This supports a mixed state—new hardware sales unavailable while at least some consumable support is still described—not a blanket assertion that every service has ended. [S-HALO-03]

## What was assumed

The sources do not expose internal product-roadmap or acquisition diligence materials, so the dossier does not attribute beliefs to Halo or Flow teams. **Editorial inference:** it would be unsafe to assume an asset acquisition preserves every legacy user promise, or that a newer device sharing a brand is a compatible successor. The official help text itself emphasizes differences between Halo Sport and the later Halo headset, while current website material describes only some continuing consumable availability.

## What the evidence suggests future teams should do differently

**Editorial inference:** tDCS hardware lifecycle is not just an electronics question. Ongoing use can depend on electrode accessories, skin-contact consumables, firmware/app compatibility, dosing instructions, safety updates and access to replacement equipment. An asset acquisition and an updated product name may preserve some capabilities without preserving every earlier product’s ecosystem.

For consumer neuromodulation, publish a dated support matrix for each hardware model: whether new units can be purchased, whether software and mobile apps are maintained, whether consumables remain compatible, how safety updates are delivered, and which entity handles defects, returns and user data. Acquirers should explicitly state which legacy products and obligations they assume and which they do not.

## Consequences

The source set documents that Halo Sport is discontinued and that some Halo consumables remained advertised for existing users. It does not establish that every owner lost access, that the original headset is technically unsafe or ineffective, that all legacy app services currently function, or that Flow assumed all warranties. This is an availability and support-boundary case, not a clinical-efficacy case.

## What remains unknown

- Whether the Halo Sport app, pairing, software and safety-update infrastructure remain functional across all supported phone operating systems.
- Exact end dates for hardware support, customer service, warranty handling and different primer generations.
- Which legal obligations, user accounts, data and consumer contracts were included in the 2021 asset transfer.
- Whether the later Halo headset is a direct technical successor to Sport; available help text emphasizes differences in target and intended use.

## Questions future teams should ask

- Which product generation does a current support article actually refer to?
- Are consumables available and compatible even if the core device is not sold?
- Did an asset sale transfer user accounts, warranty duties and software-maintenance responsibility?
- How are users told the difference between “sold out,” “discontinued,” “unsupported” and “unsafe”?
