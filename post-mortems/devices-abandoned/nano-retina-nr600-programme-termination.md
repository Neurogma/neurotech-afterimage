## Case identity and outcome

Nano Retina developed the NR600, a wireless epiretinal visual prosthesis intended to provide artificial visual perception for people with advanced retinitis pigmentosa. The public record identifies the clinical study as NCT04295304. Registry-derived trial pages report a start date of 17 January 2020, actual study completion on 12 March 2023, status “terminated,” and “lack of funds” as the reason for stopping; a 2024 clinical-industry review reports nine implantation procedures. Separately, company-ecosystem reporting dates Nano Retina’s operational cessation to August 2023, while an implant-profile page says the company closed in spring 2024. The registry timeline and company-closure reports are not identical and should be distinguished rather than collapsed into one date. This dossier is classified as **clinical-development termination and reported corporate discontinuity, with post-study support for individual recipients not established**; it does not assert patient abandonment. [S-NR-01]–[S-NR-06]

## What the public record shows

NR600 consisted of an implanted retinal chip and external glasses. Specialist reports described early human implantation and initial visual effects in a small number of participants, including performance on simple visual tasks. These reports establish that the programme reached human clinical evaluation, not that it had achieved a durable, commercially ready treatment. [S-NR-01] [S-NR-02]

Registry-derived pages for NCT04295304 report an actual study completion date of 12 March 2023, a last registry update in March 2023, terminated status and “lack of funds” as the reason for stopping. The official ClinicalTrials.gov record is linked in the source register, but its dynamic page did not expose the full history in the browser extraction used for this pass; the close-out record should be checked directly before publication. A 2024 review reports nine implantation procedures; a 2026 review labels termination as occurring in 2024, which conflicts with the registry-derived completion timeline. Separately, Startup Nation Central dates company cessation to August 2023, while the Bionic Vision implant page says spring 2024. These differences may reflect distinct milestones or secondary-source error; this dossier does not silently choose one corporate-closure date. [S-NR-02]–[S-NR-06]

## What was built

NR600 was a wireless epiretinal prosthesis combining a miniature implant with glasses that supplied near-infrared power and controlled visual input. The electrode array was intended to stimulate retinal neurons and create visual percepts for people with severe retinal degeneration. Early reports of visual effects or basic visual tasks do not establish everyday independence, sustained performance, regulatory approval or long-term safety. [S-NR-01] [S-NR-02]

## Chronology

- **17 January 2020:** Registry-derived trial pages list the study start date under NCT04295304. [S-NR-02] [S-NR-05]
- **March 2020:** The company publicly described initial implantations and activation in two participants as part of a planned European multicentre study. [S-NR-01]
- **12 March 2023:** Registry-derived trial pages list the actual study completion date; trial status was reported as terminated and the reason given as lack of funds. The official record history should be checked directly before publication. [S-NR-05]
- **August 2023 / spring 2024:** Secondary business sources report Nano Retina company-closure dates that do not agree. Another review labels trial termination as occurring in 2024, although the registry-derived completion date is in March 2023. The exact company-closure date and relationship between these milestones remain unresolved. [S-NR-03] [S-NR-04] [S-NR-06]

## What was assumed

No direct company financial records, liquidation documents or full trial close-out report were reviewed. **Editorial inference:** the cost and duration of developing an implantable visual prosthesis may have exceeded the financing available to advance beyond early human evaluation, but available reporting does not establish that this was the only cause. Nor does termination alone establish that all participants lost care.

## Consequences

The sources cited support that NR600 entered human evaluation and that the study was later reported as terminated. They do not fully establish the post-study condition of every implant, whether or when all implanted systems were explanted, the duration of participant follow-up, or whether clinical centres retained sufficient expertise and components after the sponsor's cessation. The record does not justify a statement that every participant was abandoned. [S-NR-01]–[S-NR-06]

## What the evidence suggests future teams should do differently

**Editorial inference:** before an invasive early-feasibility study begins, sponsors and investigators should document end-of-study scenarios for corporate insolvency, including contact points for each participant, follow-up responsibility, management of complications, device-status monitoring and any protocol-specific explant plan. Registry updates should distinguish the date a company ceases operations from the date a trial is terminated, and should state whether participants remain under follow-up.

## What remains unknown

- The full official history and close-out reason for NCT04295304, including the exact registry update that changed status to terminated.
- Whether all nine reported implantation procedures correspond to unique participants and what the final participant disposition was.
- Whether implants remained in place, were explanted under protocol, or received later maintenance.
- Which clinical centre or institution retained responsibility after company operations ceased.
- Whether device technical files, adverse-event records and long-term follow-up data were transferred to another organization.

## Questions future teams should ask

- Who is responsible for participants if the sponsor stops operations before the trial is closed out?
- Does the registry report the reason for termination, the number implanted, and the status of long-term follow-up clearly?
- Are device explantation and post-trial safety plans funded independently of the startup's future financing?

## Right of reply

No outreach was made to Nano Retina, trial investigators or participating hospitals for this research pass. This is an editorial draft, not a patient-level account. Before external publication, the ClinicalTrials.gov history, close-out report and centre-level follow-up arrangements should be requested or confirmed.
